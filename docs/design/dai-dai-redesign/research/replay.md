# Handoff: "The chart run, week by week" (the Dai Dai replay)

**Written 26 Sep 2026 for a Claude Design session starting cold.** Read from the
repo at `93fedb07` (`~/burnaboy-website`, read-only). Every value below is
quoted from code with `file:line`. Where the repo does not hold something, this
file says so. It does not fill the gap with a guess.

**What is being designed:**
1. A new section on `/dai-dai` (and on `/dai-dai/es`, see §8). It is an animated
   world map plus a ranking of Dai Dai's chart positions, frame by frame, with
   play/pause and a scrubber.
2. A full-screen version at `/dai-dai/replay` with a **Download video** action.
3. A static **small-multiples** view. It is used under reduced motion, without
   JavaScript, and as the accessible reading.

**The one thing to understand first:** the site stores Dai Dai's chart run as
**peaks, not runs**. At HEAD, no country has a week-by-week series in
`app/data/`. A weekly replay is designed against data that has to be transcribed
first, and it will have honest gaps. §2 has the exact count. §4 says how to
draw the gaps.

## 1. What exists in the repo, source by source

### 1a. Official national charts: peak and longevity only

`app/data/charts.ts:337-365` is the Dai Dai release block. `ChartEntry`
(`charts.ts:197-221`) has only `c`, `peak`, `note?`, `weeksAtPeak?` and
`weeks?`, with no dates. The comment at `charts.ts:203-218` explains why:
longevity is set "only where the owning body states it".

- **66 countries** (plus `GLB`, `GLBX`, which are the two Billboard global
  charts). `daiDaiChartEntryCount` = **68** and `daiDaiNumberOnes` = **26**,
  both computed from `charts.ts`.
- 23 of the 66 carry `weeksAtPeak` and/or `weeks`. The other **43 carry a peak
  and nothing else**.
- The end state of any replay has to equal this block. It is the same data the
  current "world takeover" grid reads (`app/dai-dai/page.tsx:25-35`,
  `app/components/DaiDaiConquest.tsx`).

### 1b. Official week-by-week positions: only in prose, never structured

These readings are real and were read at the chart bodies. They sit in docs,
in `note` strings and in the updates feed. No file holds them as data.

### 1c. The two worldwide daily charts: anchors, not series

- **Spotify Global Daily.** `app/data/daiDai.ts:65` gives the debut (15 May,
  No. 114). `:150-160` gives the 37 days at No. 1 in six spells (30 Jun; 3-7 Jul;
  10-14 Jul; 17-30 Jul; 7-16 Aug; 21-22 Aug). `:206-221` has some top-10
  positions after 28 Aug. The daily position is not stored for every day.
- **YouTube Global Daily Top Music Videos.** `daiDai.ts:274-344` says No. 1 on
  93 of its first 97 days. It was missed on 28-29 May, 2 Jun and 8 Jun, and the
  file does not give those positions. It was No. 2 on 5-6 Sep, No. 1 on 7 Sep
  and No. 2 on 14 Sep. **Do not display the 93** (`daiDai.ts:340-344` explains
  why it is deliberately unpublished).

### 1d. `app/data/runHistory.ts`: the only dated series in `app/data`

Generated by `scripts/build-live-charts.mjs:579-661`. It holds **worldwide rows
only**: `:590` keeps only `country === "WW"`. For Dai Dai it has 3 platforms
(Spotify, Deezer, Shazam global) with 45 readings each, **9 Aug to 26 Sep**. No
reading exists on 10 Sep or 20-22 Sep.

- **The date is the sweep day, not the chart date.** Checked against the
  walked Spotify spells: the 9-17 Aug rows read No. 1, which are the charts of
  8-16 Aug. The 18 Aug row reads No. 2, the chart of 17 Aug. The lag is
  usually one day and **sometimes two**: the rows of 1-4 Sep carry the charts
  of 30 Aug-2 Sep. Some rows repeat the previous day's value: 16-19 Sep all
  read 20, but the chart read 15 on 18 Sep and 7 on 19 Sep (`daiDai.ts:215-219`).
- Its own header says a chart "should say so rather than implying it has
  watched a run from its first day" (`build-live-charts.mjs:659-661`,
  `runHistoryDays`). The replay must follow the same rule.

### 1e. Per-country **platform** charts, daily: only in git history

`app/data/liveCharts.ts` is a snapshot of Spotify, Apple Music, iTunes, Deezer,
Shazam and YouTube country charts. It is rebuilt several times a day
(`liveCharts.ts:1-8`: "This is not official-chart data"). The file only knows
today, but **its git history is a daily per-country series**: 615 commits,
**56 snapshot days from 29 Jul to 26 Sep** (none on 10 Sep or 20-22 Sep).

I reconstructed it read-only, taking the last commit of each UTC day, and saved
it for you to design against:
**`~/burnaboy-work/daidai-handoff/replay-platform-daily.json`** (Spotify, Apple
Music and YouTube).

| Platform | Countries per day (excluding WW) | Countries at No. 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Spotify (daily) | 67 on 29 Jul, 68 max (2 Aug), 35 on 26 Sep | 9 on 29 Jul, 11 max (31 Jul), 1 on 26 Sep |
| Apple Music (daily) | 108 on 29 Jul, 64 on 26 Sep | 20 on 29 Jul, 0 on 26 Sep |
| YouTube (**weekly**, `app/lib/liveChartMeta.ts:17`) | 121 on 29 Jul, 104 on 26 Sep | 62 on 29 Jul, 21 on 26 Sep |

Things to know about this data:
- It starts **75 days after release**, after most countries had already
  peaked. On 29 Jul Spotify already had it at No. 1 in AT, BE, DE, NL, NO, PT,
  SE, CH and AE.
- Spotify writes the UK as `GB`. Every other platform writes `UK`. Spotify and
  Deezer also carry a `WW` row.
- Whole-platform snapshots identical to the previous day, which means stale
  source reads: Spotify 9 days, Deezer 6, Shazam 8.
- 59 of the 66 official-chart countries appear on Spotify at least once. 33
  appear on all 56 days. SR, LB, PR, HR, RU, MD and VN never appear.
- Spotify also lists countries that have **no official row**: CY, MA, HK, TW,
  DO, MX, PK, BY and SV. DO and SV were removed as false No. 1 claims
  (`updates.ts:662`). A platform layer must never read as official.
  - House rule: never mix platform placements into official-chart data.
  - House rule: prefer the owner's own tracking over automated kworb pulls for
    streaming figures. That makes this a labelled secondary layer at most.

## 3. The data shape the component reads (proposed, does not exist yet)

This would be a new generated or hand-kept file, e.g. `app/data/daiDaiRuns.ts`.
Nothing in it is typed into prose. Peaks and counts are read from `charts.ts`,
never retyped.

```ts
type Family  = "official" | "platform";       // never shown on one layer together
type Cadence = "weekly" | "daily";
type Status  = "on" | "off" | "unread" | "no-chart";
//  on       = read, on the chart at `pos`
//  off      = read, not on the chart (before debut / after exit)
//  unread   = no reading exists in our records  <- the common case
//  no-chart = the body published no chart that week (PA 16 Jul; GR summer 31-34 combined)

interface RunPoint {
  frame: string;     // ISO date of the frame this reading belongs to (see below)
  chartDate: string; // the body's OWN date for that issue
  label?: string;    // the body's own week label: "semaine 28", "v33", "W34", "31-34 (combined)"
  status: Status;
  pos?: number;      // only when status === "on"
  source: string;    // doc path / URL, shown in the tooltip footnote
}

interface CountryRun {
  code: string;        // A2 as CHART_COUNTRIES uses it (UK, not GB); "GLB"/"GLBX" for the globals
  iso?: number;        // A2_TO_ISO (app/lib/isoCodes.ts:5-16); absent for GLB/GLBX
  body: string;        // CHART_COUNTRIES[code].body
  family: Family;
  cadence: Cadence;
  peak: number;        // === charts.ts peak (test-enforced)
  weeksAtPeak?: number; weeks?: number;  // from charts.ts
  points: RunPoint[];  // [] = peak-only country
}

interface ReplayFrame {        // derived at build time, not stored
  frame: string;               // e.g. "2026-07-13"
  ranked: { code: string; pos: number }[];   // status "on" only, sorted by pos
  unread: string[];            // codes with no reading this frame
  numberOnes: number;          // counts READ No. 1s only
}
```

**The frame axis** is about 20 weekly frames, from the week of release
(15 May, `daiDai.ts:65`) to the week of 26 Sep (the latest issues held: LU,
GLB, GLBX). A daily layer would be 135 frames over the same span, or 60 frames
(29 Jul to 26 Sep) for the platform data. The bodies date their weeks
differently:

- Sunday-dated Swiss issues
- ISO week numbers in SE, PL, GR and SK
- SNEP's "semaine du" dates
- Billboard issue dates that run ahead of tracking: the charts "dated 22
  August" cover the "7-13 August tracking week" (`updates.ts:884`)

So a frame is a **calendar week**, and the tooltip always shows the body's own
date and label.

**Invariants the builder should test:**
- The final frame's best-ever positions equal `charts.ts` peaks for all 66.
- No point is invented: `unread` is never filled from a neighbour.
- `numberOnes` never exceeds 26.
- EN and ES render the same frames.

## 4. What to design

### 4.1 Anatomy (desktop, 1240px and up, inside `.wrap` max-width 1280px, `app/dai-dai/dai-dai.module.css:7`)

- **Header.** Use the page's kicker plus Anton h2 pattern
  (`dai-dai.module.css:10-42`). "Chart run · week by week" is a placeholder.
- **World strip.** GLB and GLBX (plus, if the daily layer ships, Spotify Global
  and YouTube Global) are **not countries** and have no shape. Give them a
  strip of 2-4 tiles above the map, each showing its position this frame.
- **Map plus Europe inset.** Map about 2/3 width, ranking about 1/3.
  - Geometry: `app/data/worldShapes.ts`, Equal Earth 900×470 viewBox
    (`:1-8`), keyed by ISO numeric.
  - **The No. 1 strongholds are tiny.** Long side in viewBox units: LU 1.2,
    LB 4.8, PR 4.0, NL 7.4, BE 7.5, PT 15.9, CH 9.3, IE 8.6, DK 9.1, EE 9.1,
    CZ 13.8, SK 11.7, AT 15.5, DE 19.9.
  - At a 358px phone map (scale 0.4) CH is about 3.7px and LU about half a
    pixel. **SG has no shape at all.** That is why the flag grid replaced the
    old map (`DaiDaiConquest.tsx:6-18`). A **Europe inset is mandatory**, and
    SG needs a marker or dot treatment.
- **Ranking.** Countries with a reading this frame, sorted by position. Design
  for **up to 26 sharing No. 1** in one frame; the real weekly maximum is lower
  and becomes known once the runs are transcribed. Group ties rather than
  inventing an order. Show a secondary sort cue (weeks at No. 1 so far).
  Include an "unread this week" tray under the list (see 4.4).
- **Transport.** Play/pause, a scrubber with week ticks, step back/forward,
  and the date readout ("Week of 13 July 2026"). Optional event markers:
  release 15 May and the halftime show 19 Jul (`page.tsx:135`). Both are
  sourced on the page.
- **Counter.** "No. 1 in N countries this week" (read ones only), plus
  "M not read".
- **Footnote.** Source families and the "frames are calendar weeks; each
  country's chart dates its own week" line. When the platform layer is on:
  "Platform charts, not official charts".

### 4.2 States

| State | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Poster / initial** | The END frame (every peak). It is server-rendered, so no-JS readers and crawlers get the finished picture. Same principle as the FAQ at `page.tsx:439-461`. A clear Play affordance. **No autoplay.** |
| **Playing** | Frames advance, around 0.8-1.2 s per weekly frame, so about 20 s end to end. Map fills cross-fade and ranking rows re-order. The live region is silent. |
| **Paused** | The frame holds. Hover or focus on a country shows a card: body name, the body's date and label, position or status, and source. The live region announces the frame once. |
| **Scrubbing** | Dragging snaps to whole frames with no tweening. The readout updates live. Keyboard: arrow keys step one frame, Home/End jump, Space plays or pauses. The scrubber's `aria-valuetext` is e.g. "Week of 13 July 2026: No. 1 in 9 countries, 12 not read". |
| **End** | Settles on the peak picture and reads the same as the takeover grid (26 of 66 at No. 1). Offer Replay. Peak-only countries get their treatment here (4.4). |
| **Video** (full-screen page) | Idle → rendering (progress plus cancel) → ready (download) → failed or unsupported (plain message, the page still plays). |

### 4.3 Legend: position encoding

The existing tokens are data colours, which "must never be recoloured to gold"
(`app/globals.css:230-233`). The discrete five-step ramp suits frame-to-frame
reading. Values are `light-dark(LIGHT, DARK)` (`globals.css:243-247`,
thresholds from `app/records/visualized/page.tsx:251-255`):

| Band | Token | Light | Dark |
|---|---|---|---|
| No. 1 | `--peak-band-1` | `#57360a` | `#ffd24a` |
| Top 5 | `--peak-band-5` | `#945e00` | `#ffb627` |
| Top 10 | `--peak-band-10` | `#b3822f` | `#c98a2e` |
| Top 40 | `--peak-band-40` | `#b4ada0` | `#8a7a52` |
| 41+ | `--peak-band-rest` | `#dad5cb` | `#5a5a62` |

The alternative is PeakMap's continuous ramp (`app/components/PeakMap.tsx:23-49`,
legend gradient `:72-78`, ticks "No. 1 / Top 10 / Top 40 / 41+" at `:150-153`).

**Watch out in light mode:** the uncharted map fill is `--bg-soft-2`
`#efeae1` (`PeakMap.module.css:12-15`, `globals.css:25`). The band ramp runs
**2.85, 1.86 and 1.22 to 1** against it for Top 10, Top 40 and 41+
(`globals.css:238-242`). A 41+ country is nearly invisible on paper, so Top 40
and 41+ need a second cue, such as an outline stroke.

**Non-position states.** Each needs its own swatch and must work in both
themes:
1. **Not yet charted / off chart (read)**: the uncharted fill.
2. **No reading this week**: a hatch over the last-known band is **not
   allowed**, because it implies a position. Use a neutral hatch with a dashed
   outline.
3. **No chart published this week** (PA 16 Jul, GR summer): distinct from
   "unread", because the body published nothing.
4. **Peak only, run not recorded**: see 4.4.
5. **Platform layer on**: the whole map carries a visible "platform" marker
   (label or tint). It must never look like the official layer.

### 4.4 Gaps

- **Never interpolate and never carry the last reading forward.** The site
  does not publish a figure nobody read (`daiDai.ts:289-297`, `:312-316`).
- **Map:** an unread week gets the "no reading" hatch.
- **Ranking:** unread countries leave the ranked list and sit in the "not read
  this week" tray (flags or codes, muted). They don't get ranked slots.
- **Counter:** read No. 1s only, with "+M not read".
- **Peak-only countries (43 of 66, plus 8 with counts but no dates).** During
  play they sit in a quiet "charted: run not recorded" state, a thin dotted
  outline with no fill. **At the end frame** they fill with their peak band and
  a small "peak" mark. This keeps the end frame equal to the takeover grid
  without implying a date.

### 4.6 Reduced motion and no-JS: the small-multiples view

- **The global reduced-motion rule does not stop a JS animation.**
  `globals.css:1693-1700` only zeroes CSS animation and transition durations,
  so the component must check `matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)")`
  itself.
- **Under reduced motion,** render the small multiples by default and never
  autoplay. The player is available only on an explicit press, and even then it
  steps frames without tweening.
- **The small multiples:** one row per chart, 20 week-cells, each cell filled
  with its band or a status swatch.
  - Rows are ordered by peak, then by weeks at peak. The two global charts
    come first.
  - Peak-only rows show a single "peak #N, run not recorded" cell at the right.
  - This is also the no-JS reading and the screen-reader table: caption,
    column headers = weeks, row headers = countries.
  - It is 66 or more rows, so design its phone density. Keep it dense, not an
    accordion.
