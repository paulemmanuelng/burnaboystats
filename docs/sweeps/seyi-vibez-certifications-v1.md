The cap is real and was tested, not assumed: `/_next/data/{buildId}/certification.json` returns the same 500 rows, `?limit=5000` changes nothing, and `/api/certification` and `/api/certifications` both 404. The rows live in `props.pageProps.certEntries` inside a ~10 MB `__NEXT_DATA__` payload — **a plain `curl` without `--compressed` truncates at ~8.4 MB and silently drops rows**, which is the single easiest way to get this register wrong.

**Dates in this register are not award dates.** Every live row reads `2026-02-06`. The register's own dated batches (2023-06-08, 2024-10-04, 2024-10-14, 2025-01-05, 2025-02-06, 2025-04-04, 2026-02-06) are bulk re-dates. Do not publish any of them as the date a plaque was awarded.

## Known gaps

1. **Seven of the 46 TurnTable captures could not be parsed** — `20230929234834`, `20231129022850`, `20240226113805`, `20240529152014`, `20241204155343`, `20250214052135` (each crawler-truncated at exactly 1,048,576 bytes) and `20260610130030` (truncated at exactly 5,242,880 bytes). Every one amputates the trailing `__NEXT_DATA__`. This almost certainly costs nothing, and the reason is demonstrable: each register era has exactly **one** distinct row set across all its parsed captures (103-row era: 12 captures, 1 row set; 206-row era: 13 captures, 1 row set; 500-row era: 5 captures, 1 row set), and every truncated capture sits inside one of those eras. **It is still a gap, not a zero.**
2. **No capture exists between 21 Feb 2026 (2,477 rows) and 30 Apr 2026 (500 rows)** — precisely the window in which the register was cut down to its capped page. Anything TCSN awarded in that window, or in any window Wayback missed, is unrecoverable. The register's own newest batch date is 2026-02-06.
3. **RiSA's published register stops at 09 Sep 2025.** Any South African certification issued in the eleven months since is not in the register that was read and cannot be checked.
4. **Provenance flag on RiSA's row count.** 608 rows were read this pass; an earlier sweep in this series recorded **748 rows reaching 08.06.2026**. The register has either shrunk or is being served differently, and this was not resolved. **Re-check before anyone quotes RiSA row counts.** The Seyi Vibez finding is unaffected — his entire career (2021→) sits inside the window that was read, and the live search endpoint independently returns nothing for him.
5. **"No other African certifying body exists" is a literature judgement, not a register read.** TCSN and RiSA were confirmed to be the only two African registers with a public database, and GHAMRO, MUSIGA and the RCN domains were probed individually. Collecting societies in Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Egypt, Morocco, Côte d'Ivoire and Senegal were **not** individually probed; none is documented as running a sales-certification programme, but that is not the same as having read one.
6. **The non-African half of the certification sweep returned zero plaques, and this file carries its result but not its per-body evidence.** RIAA, BPI, SNEP, Music Canada, RMNZ, FIMI, MAHASZ, NVPI, Ultratop, IRMA, AFP and the rest are therefore recorded here as **"no plaque returned"**, not as registers this file can show read row by row. Under the house standard that is a gap in the *evidence trail*, not a proven zero, and it should be closed by a direct read before anyone publishes "Seyi Vibez has no UK/US/EU certification" as a proven fact. What can be said today, and is enough for the headline: **every plaque that any register returned for him is Nigerian.**

### Context

Under this identical standard, **Burna Boy holds 230 certifications across 26 countries** ([burnaboystats.com](https://burnaboystats.com)). Elsewhere in this sweep series: **Wizkid 155**, **Tems 70**.

Seyi Vibez's **103** is the third-largest total in the series and the most lopsided by a distance: it is a **single-market** figure. Burna Boy's 230 spans 26 countries; Seyi Vibez's 103 spans one. Read as a per-country number, though, it is extraordinary — 103 Nigerian plaques is more Nigerian certifications than any other artist swept in this series carries, and it is built almost entirely from singles the international registers have never seen. His top plaque, *Cana* at **6× Platinum**, is the highest single-title tier found for any artist in this sweep outside Burna Boy's own file.

The honest reading is that his certification career and his streaming career occupy the same country. That is not a weakness in the data — it is the finding. And the second finding is methodological: **half of it is invisible on the register's own website**, and would stay invisible to anyone who trusted the live page.
