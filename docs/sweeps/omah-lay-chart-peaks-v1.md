# Omah Lay — every official chart peak (full chart-body sweep)

**Standard:** identical to burnaboystats.com. One entry per **release per country**, on that country's **principal national singles or albums chart**. **Both singles and albums count**, recorded separately. **Lead, co-billed and featured credits all count**, on the chart body's own printed credit line. *peak* = the highest position the release has ever reached on that chart. **Billboard Global 200 and Global 200 Excl. US count as their own two entries** (GLB / GLBX). Where a country runs two principal charts, the **better** peak is that country's single entry.

**Airplay carve-out:** an airplay chart counts **only** where a country publishes no non-airplay national chart. Nigeria, South Africa, Germany, Spain, Poland, Croatia and Romania all have better airplay peaks that are **not** used.

**Excluded throughout:** platform charts (Spotify, Apple Music, iTunes, Deezer, Shazam, YouTube, Audiomack, Boomplay); genre and component charts (Afrobeats Songs, Streaming Songs, Radio Songs, repertoire splits); extension charts sitting below a main chart (US Bubbling Under, NZ Hot Singles, UK 101–200, Ultratip, TurnTable Bubbling Under); year-end and mid-week charts; supranational regional charts (Official MENA Chart, Official North Africa Chart); and certifications.

Swept **19 August 2026** in two halves — Nigeria/Africa/Billboard Global, and rest of world — then reconciled. **240,983 + 210,900 chart rows read at source.**

## Known gaps

An unread chart is a gap, not a zero. No peak is claimed for anything in this section.

1. **🇳🇬 Nigeria is a floor, not a ceiling.** TurnTable serves only the current week; everything historical comes from Wayback. **77 of roughly 245 singles issues and 48 of roughly 200 albums issues** were read. Because the register publishes `highestPosition` (career peak-to-date) on every row, one capture while a title is still charting recovers that title's full peak — which is why 38 singles and 4 albums are recoverable from 125 issues. **What cannot be recovered is a title that entered *and* left entirely inside a coverage gap.** There are **14 gaps longer than three weeks** on the singles side, the worst being 2022-11-24 → 2023-03-16 (16 weeks) and, on albums, 2025-07-24 → 2025-12-18 (21 weeks).
2. **🇳🇬 Nigeria before July 2022 is unreadable.** His breakout era — *Bad Influence*, *Godly*, *Lo Lo*, *You*, *Confession*, the Get Layd and What Have We Done cycles of 2020–21 — fell under the **TurnTable Top 50**, the flagship's predecessor. ### Context

Under this identical standard, **Burna Boy has 280 chart entries and 48 No. 1 placements across 71 chart territories** ([burnaboystats.com](https://burnaboystats.com)).

The honest comparison: Omah Lay's **70 entries across 16 chart territories** is **25% of Burna Boy's entry count and 23% of his territory reach**, and the gap in No. 1s is wider still — **6 to 48**, and all six of his are in one country.

But the two lines are not measuring the same career. Burna Boy's 280 is built on a fifteen-year catalogue with more than a dozen records that charted in ten-plus countries each. Omah Lay is, on this evidence, **a Nigeria-dominant artist with almost no global chart presence**: 42 of 70 entries are Nigerian, and his entire non-Nigerian footprint outside the two guest-billed French rap hits and one Luciano feature is thin enough to list in a sentence. Across **123,600 Billboard Global rows** he appears **once**, for two weeks, at 73 and 74, on a Justin Bieber duet — and he has never entered the Hot 100 or the Billboard 200 at all.

Where the line is genuinely strong is **at home and in France**. In Nigeria he holds 38 charting singles, 4 charting albums and 6 No. 1 placements, with *Boy Alone* still on the albums chart at **197 weeks** — a run that outlasts most of what this sweep set contains. In France he reached **No. 2** on SNEP, higher than anything Ayra Starr, Tems or Tyla have managed on that chart, and did it as a **featured guest on a Jul record** — the kind of placement no artist-side fan list would ever surface. His Nigerian count is also the one most likely to be a floor: an entire breakout era, 2020–21, sits behind an unreadable predecessor chart, and TurnTable publishes no weekly archive at all.
