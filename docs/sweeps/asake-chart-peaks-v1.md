# Asake — every official chart peak (full chart-body sweep)

**Standard:** identical to burnaboystats.com. One entry per **release per country**, on that country's **principal national singles or albums chart**. **Both singles and albums count**, recorded separately. **Lead, co-billed and featured credits all count.** *peak* = the highest position the release has ever reached on that chart. **Billboard Global 200 and Global 200 Excl. US count as their own two entries** (codes GLB / GLBX). Where a country runs two principal charts, the **better** peak is reported as that country's single entry.

**Airplay carve-out:** an airplay chart counts **only** where a country publishes no non-airplay national chart. **Nigeria publishes a non-airplay national chart, so it is not a carve-out country** — the TurnTable Official Radio Songs peaks are excluded, however quotable they are.

**Excluded throughout:** platform charts (Spotify, Apple Music, iTunes, Deezer, Shazam, YouTube, Audiomack, Boomplay); genre and component charts (Afrobeats Songs, Streaming Songs, Radio Songs, Afro-Pop, repertoire splits); extension charts sitting below a main chart (US Bubbling Under, TurnTable Bubbling Under, NZ Hot Singles, UK 101–200); year-end and mid-week charts; recurrent charts; artist-ranking charts; supranational regional charts (IFPI MENA / North Africa); and certifications.

**Sweep run 19 Aug 2026**, in two halves — Nigeria + Africa + the Billboard charts, and the non-African national bodies.

**How Nigeria was read — and a standing claim overturned.** Every Nigerian row comes from TurnTable's own Next.js chart payload, which carries a per-row **`highestPosition`** field: the chart body's own record of the career peak, not a peak inferred from sampling. Sources: the live payload at `turntablecharts.com/charts/1` and `/charts/2` (19 Aug 2026) **plus 298 Wayback captures** of those two URLs and of the legacy `/Charts/Top50` URL. Net coverage: **130 distinct singles-chart weeks** (7 Jul 2022 – 19 Aug 2026, 60% of the 215 published weeks) and **89 distinct albums-chart weeks** (2 Nov 2022 – 19 Aug 2026, 45% of 199) — **28,900 chart rows**.

This **overturns the standing claim in the Rema, Wizkid, Tyla and Ayra Starr files that "TurnTable exposes no weekly archive."** The live endpoint serves exactly one week, which is what those sweeps observed — but the Wayback Machine holds roughly **280 readable weekly payloads**. That correction should propagate: it means the 23 Ayra Starr Nigerian rows recorded as "unverified by construction" are, in fact, verifiable.

**How South Africa was read — and a second standing claim overturned.** `theofficialsacharts.co.za`'s catch-all route serves machine-generated filler prose, which is why earlier sweeps concluded the site was dead. **The exact weekly chart paths return the real tables, with a PEAK column.** 139 weekly issues read in full — 2022 44/52 (Top 100 era), 2023 34/52, 2024 51/52, 2025 10, plus 32 Wayback captures — **5,446 chart rows**.

## Known gaps

**Read this before quoting any figure below. An unread chart is a gap, not a zero.**

1. **🇳🇬 THE TURNTABLE TOP 50 ERA IS UNREADABLE AT SOURCE — and it costs Asake more than any other artist in this family.** The Official Nigeria Top 100 launched with the week of 1–7 Jul 2022 (published 11 Jul 2022, instance 1146); in that capture **all 100 rows read `weeksOnChart=1` and `highestPosition=rank`** — every counter reset. Nigeria's principal chart before that date was the **TurnTable Top 50**, and its weekly tables cannot be recovered: all eight surviving Wayback captures of `/Charts/Top50` from Nov 2020 to Jun 2022 are **Nuxt pages that fetched their data client-side and archived with an empty payload**. Consequence: **every Asake release that charted before 11 Jul 2022 carries a Top-100-era FLOOR, not a peak.** Two are corrected from TurnTable's own editorial (*Sungba (Remix)* → No. 1, *Omo Ope* → No. 1); the rest are left at their floors and flagged ‡ — ***Palazzo* #5** (press reports a Top 50 debut at **No. 2** on 23 May 2022, which is **not** a register read and is therefore **not used**), ***Trabaye* #74**, and the ***Ololade Asake – EP* #15** (the albums chart did not exist until Nov 2022 at all).
2. **🇳🇬 The Nigerian coverage is a floor in a second, independent way.** `highestPosition` gives the true career peak for any release actually seen, so **no recorded peak is understated by sampling**. But a release that charted *only* inside the **85 singles weeks and 110 album weeks that could not be retrieved** would be missing from this file entirely. **The 94 Nigerian entries are a minimum.**
3. **🇿🇦 TOSAC is 139 of 241 weeks.** 2024 is near-complete (51/52) and 2022 is 44/52, but **2025 is only 10 weeks and 2026 none** — the site began serving filler under load and would not finish. Those are the Top 20 / Top 50 eras, where an Asake entry is improbable but **unproven**. Eight 2022 weeks (43, 44, 47–52) appear never to have been published at all: no URL pattern at any depth resolves. One archived 2022 week (18) is Wayback-truncated at 1 MB and yielded 73 of 100 rows.
4. **🇳🇬 Two No. 1s rest on TurnTable editorial rather than a chart table.** *Sungba (Remix)* and *Omo Ope* are recorded at No. 1 on the strength of `turntablecharts.com/news/578` — **the chart body's own publication**, not a secondary source, but not a retrievable ranking either. They are **flagged †, not silently promoted**. A stricter reading of this file's own standard would drop the No. 1 total from 24 to 22.
5. **TurnTable's article 578 is internally inconsistent.** It says **eleven** songs topped a Nigerian chart in the first three quarters of 2022, then names **ten**, while separately naming *Omo Ope* as one of the No. 1s. This file follows the **naming**, not the arithmetic — and records the discrepancy rather than hiding it.
6. **The non-African half is a floor, not an exhaustive world sweep.** Nine national bodies were read and produced entries or evidenced zeros. **Germany, Netherlands, Italy, Spain, Australia, New Zealand, the Nordics, Poland, Japan, South Korea and the Latin American charts were not individually swept for Asake this pass.** One strong indirect signal exists and is worth stating precisely: a full control read of **Central Cee's own Hung Medien chart matrix (DE / CH / AT / FR / NL / BE / IT / ES / PT / SE / NO / FI / DK / AU / NZ) contains no "Wave" row** — which rules that *single* out across fifteen territories, and rules out nothing else.
7. **🇨🇦 Canada's zero is evidenced; the rest of the Americas is not.** All 242 weekly Billboard Canadian Hot 100 issues (08.01.2022 – 22.08.2026) were read and *Active* at #95 is the **only** Asake singles row in the entire span. No equivalent full-run read was done for Latin America.

### Context

Under this identical standard, **Burna Boy has 280 chart entries and 48 No. 1 placements across 71 chart territories** ([burnaboystats.com](https://burnaboystats.com)).

Asake's **123 entries across 11 chart territories** is **44% of Burna Boy's entry count** and **half his No. 1 tally** — but only **15% of his chart-territory reach**, and that last number is the honest one. The two lines are not the same shape at all. Burna Boy's 280 is spread across 71 territories at an average of four entries each; Asake's 123 is **94 entries in one country** plus a 29-entry tail.

Set against the others in this sweep, the comparison is sharper still. Ayra Starr has **78 entries across 30 chart territories** — fewer entries than Asake, in nearly three times as many places. Asake out-charts her at home by 94 to 28 and out-No.-1s her 24 to 10, and then loses the international comparison outright: she has a Spanish No. 1, a six-week Peruvian No. 1 and a Bolivian No. 1; he has **no No. 1 anywhere outside Nigeria, and a best Billboard Global 200 peak of #120.**

Three things in this file are genuinely remarkable and none of them is international. **Twenty-four Nigerian No. 1 placements** is the largest home-market tally recorded in this family. **Every full-length he has released has topped the albums chart** — five studio albums and a joint EP, no exceptions. And *Lonely At The Top* was read at rank 1 in **eleven separate archived weeks**, the longest No. 1 run anyone in this sweep has evidenced week by week.

What the data does not support is the crossover story. There is **no Hot 100 entry and no Billboard 200 entry, ever** — both proven across 242 weekly issues rather than merely unfound. His best global figure is *Jogodo* at **#75 on Global Excl. U.S.**, on a Wizkid-led record. In South Africa, the continent's other major chart market, **one release has ever charted: *Bandana*, at #79, for six weeks in 2022.** And *Active* — the Travis Scott record — went **No. 1 on a US genre chart while never entering the Hot 100 at all**, which is the single cleanest illustration of where this catalogue sits: enormous at home, visible in the UK, and not yet a fixture anywhere else.
