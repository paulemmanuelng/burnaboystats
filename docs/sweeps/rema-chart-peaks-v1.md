# Rema — Official Chart Peak History

**Standard:** One entry per release per country, taken from that country's **principal national singles or albums chart**. Both singles and albums count and are recorded separately; **lead, featured and joint credits all count**; `peak` is the highest position the release has ever reached on that chart. Billboard Global 200 and Billboard Global Excl. U.S. count as their own two entries. Where a country runs two principal charts (Belgium's two language sides, the Netherlands' Single Top 100 and Nederlandse Top 40, Malaysia's IFPI and Billboard charts, **Singapore's RIAS chart and Billboard Singapore Songs**, the Philippines' and Thailand's IFPI and Billboard charts, Japan's Billboard and Oricon), the **better peak** is reported as that country's single entry. **Airplay carve-out:** an airplay chart counts only where the country publishes no non-airplay national chart — the TopHit territories (RU, UA, KZ, MD, EE, BY), Bulgaria, the Monitor Latino / Record Report / PRODUCE markets, Radiomonitor's Malta, Serbia, Slovenia and Türkiye, and Lebanon's OLT20. Where a country runs both, the non-airplay chart wins every time, which is why Poland, Spain, Croatia, Romania and Nigeria have better airplay peaks that are **not** used. **Excluded everywhere:** platform charts (Spotify, Apple Music, iTunes, Deezer, Shazam, YouTube, Audiomack, Boomplay), genre and component charts (Afrobeats Songs, Streaming Songs, Radio Songs, Video Streaming, Recurrents, Circle's Download / Streaming / BGM / Retail Album lists, AFP's two streaming sections, the Monitor Latino "Anglo" lists), extension charts sitting below a main chart (US Bubbling Under, NZ Hot Singles, UK 101–200, Ultratip, NL Single Tip), year-end and mid-week charts, supranational regional charts that are not national (the IFPI MENA and North Africa charts), and certifications.

**Co-principal charts — the stated tie-break.** Where two bodies publish an all-repertoire national chart for the same country, neither outranks the other by pedigree; the **better peak of the two is taken as that country's single entry**. This is now applied uniformly to Malaysia, Singapore, the Philippines, Thailand, Japan, the Netherlands and Belgium. It is the reason Singapore is recorded at **#11** (Billboard Singapore Songs) rather than **#12** (RIAS The Official Singapore Chart) — the previous edition preferred RIAS on an unstated "industry body wins" rule that the same files did not apply in Malaysia, the Philippines or Thailand. That inconsistency is now resolved in favour of the better-peak rule, and Singapore is still one entry, not two.

**Repertoire-split ruling (applied uniformly).** Three countries publish singles charts split by repertoire rather than one combined list: Greece (IFPI Digital Singles — Local / International), India (IMI — domestic / International Top 20) and Malaysia (RIM — Malay / Chinese / International). One rule now governs all three: **a repertoire split is treated like Belgium's language split — the operative side is the national chart — but only where the country publishes no combined all-repertoire national chart. Where a combined chart exists, the repertoire list is a component chart and loses.** Greece and India have no combined chart, so their International sides count. Malaysia does have combined charts (the IFPI Official Malaysia Chart and Billboard Malaysia Songs), so RIM's International-only list is excluded and Malaysia is recorded from Billboard Malaysia Songs instead. The same test removes **Bulgaria**: the only available PROPHON figure is from its *World Top 10* international list, a repertoire component of the PROPHON Top 40, so Bulgaria yields no countable entry and drops out of the tables entirely.

**Sweep date: 17 August 2026.** This edition merges the earlier per-region sweep with a full A–Z country sweep, and incorporates the audit repair pass of the same date (see *Audit repairs*, below).

## Deliberately excluded

The numbers above are defensible because of what is *not* in them. Each of these is a real, quotable placement that fails a stated rule.

**Rule-audit corrections applied in this edition**

- **🇧🇬 Bulgaria — *Calm Down* #2 (PROPHON): REMOVED, and Bulgaria yields no countable entry.** Bulgaria is a genuine airplay carve-out country, but the #2 figure comes from PROPHON's *World Top 10* — an international-repertoire component of the PROPHON Top 40, not the principal chart. No PROPHON Top 40 peak is available.
- **🇲🇾 Malaysia — RIM International Top 20 #5: REPLACED by Billboard Malaysia Songs #8.** Same ruling: Malaysia publishes combined all-repertoire charts, so RIM's International-only list is a component.
- **🇸🇬 Singapore — RIAS #12: SUPERSEDED by Billboard Singapore Songs #11.** Singapore runs two co-principal all-repertoire charts, and the standard takes the better peak of two co-principal charts as one entry. The previous edition preferred RIAS on an "industry body outranks Billboard" rule that was never stated and was not applied in Malaysia, the Philippines or Thailand. Still one Singapore entry, now at the correct number.
- **🇮🇳 India #1 and 🇬🇷 Greece #4 / #18: RETAINED**, because neither country publishes any combined all-repertoire chart, making the International side the operative national chart in the way Ultratop's Wallonia side is for Belgium. ### Context

Under this identical standard, **Burna Boy has 280 chart entries and 48 No. 1 placements across 71 chart countries** ([burnaboystats.com](https://burnaboystats.com)).

The honest comparison: Rema's **118 entries across 53 territories** is about **42% of Burna Boy's entry count** and **75% of his territorial reach**, with **17 No. 1s against 48** — roughly a third. The gap is one of depth, not of ceiling. Rema's single best record outperforms anything in the comparison set on the Billboard Global Excl. U.S. chart, where *Calm Down* spent a week at No. 1 and 137 weeks on the chart, and his Hot 100 and UK peaks of No. 3 are both strong. But that is one song. Of his 53 territories, **50 were entered by *Calm Down***, and **29 by no other Rema release at all**; only **24** territories are reachable without it. Burna Boy's 280 entries are spread across a deep catalogue of charting albums and singles; Rema's album footprint is five entries for *HEIS* and six for *Rave & Roses*, and his non-*Calm Down* singles rarely clear the top 50 in any Western market — *Secondhand*, his second-strongest international record, peaks at #18 (Greece) and sits in the 30s–90s everywhere else. Only *Bubalu*, on a Feid co-billing, opens a region (Andean and Spanish-language markets) that *Calm Down* did not.

Two structural caveats before anyone treats the ratio as a like-for-like career verdict. First, Rema's Nigerian line is unusually strong — 7 Nigerian No. 1 singles plus 2 No. 1 albums, from 22 Nigerian entries — but Nigerian coverage is capped by TurnTable's lack of a public archive, so that count is a floor. Second, Rema is roughly a decade behind on catalogue: 280 entries reflect far more release years, and *Calm Down* is only four years old with several of its runs still open.
